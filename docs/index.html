Ask from anywhere

Press Ctrl Shift H, or Cmd Shift H on a Mac, and a little piece of Hark's sky opens near the top of your screen, over whatever you are doing.

Type and press Enter: it goes straight to Hark, and Hark opens on the reply. Rather look first? One switch in Settings makes Enter draft it instead.

Waiting in your tray

Closing the window hides Hark instead of quitting, so it is back in a blink. An orange dot shows when something is unread, and while a plan runs Hark's sky fills the icon.

On macOS it lives in the menu bar. On Linux it quits when you close it, unless you turn on the tray in settings.

Told the moment it is done

You get a notification when Hark replies. Your own messages never notify.

While Hark works on something you asked, a small island at the top of your screen shows what it is doing, then tucks away into a thin line of sky. When Hark is done it says so, with Open.

Questions

Is this the official Hark app?

No. It is an independent open source project and is not affiliated with Hark. It opens the real hark.com in its own window and adds desktop features around it. Hark and the Hark logo belong to their owners.

What can the app see?

Only what a browser tab on hark.com can see. You sign in on Hark's own pages, so the app never handles your password. The Hark page can send the app a few small events, such as “show a notification”, and the app checks each one before acting. It cannot read your files or run commands.

Why does my computer warn me when I open it?

The builds are not code-signed, and signing costs money every year. Windows shows a SmartScreen notice: choose More info, then Run anyway. On macOS, open the app with a right click and choose Open. You can also build it yourself from the source.

Why is it using several hundred megabytes?

Almost all of that is the web view that draws hark.com, which is the same engine your browser uses. The app itself is about 13 MB. Hark animates its sky, so the page is never fully idle while the window is on screen. Hidden in the tray or minimized, the page stays awake for notifications but pauses its animations, and the app uses about 2 to 3% of one core.

Does it update itself?

Not yet. Download a new release to update. Hark's own features update by themselves, because the app loads the live site.

How do I remove it?

On Windows, use Settings, Apps, Installed apps. On macOS, drag the app to the Bin. On Linux, remove the package or delete the AppImage.
